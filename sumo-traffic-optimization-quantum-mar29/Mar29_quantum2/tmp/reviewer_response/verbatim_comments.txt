The manuscript presents an interesting integration of pressure-based traffic optimization with the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), and the pressure-based energy formulation constitutes the principal scientific contribution of the work. The mathematical mapping from traffic pressure to an Ising Hamiltonian is elegant and internally consistent. However, the current experimental design does not yet provide sufficient evidence to conclude that the reported improvements arise specifically from the use of QAOA rather than from more general properties of global optimization. Several additional analyses and controls would substantially strengthen the manuscript and improve the scientific rigor of the conclusions.

The most significant methodological concern is that the current comparison simultaneously changes two experimental variables. The classical controller performs local optimization, whereas the QAOA controller performs global optimization over the entire network-wide Ising objective. Consequently, the observed performance improvements cannot be uniquely attributed to the quantum optimization algorithm because optimization scope (local versus global) and optimization strategy (classical versus QAOA) change simultaneously. The present design therefore contains an important confounding variable. A scientifically stronger comparison would include a classical global optimizer that minimizes exactly the same network-wide Ising Hamiltonian as the QAOA controller. Appropriate benchmarks include exhaustive enumeration (for the current network), simulated annealing, tabu search, branch-and-bound, mixed-integer optimization, or another established global optimization algorithm. Such comparisons would isolate the incremental contribution of the QAOA search strategy itself rather than demonstrating only that global optimization outperforms local optimization.

This issue is particularly important because the current experiments are performed on a 2×2 traffic network comprising only four intersections, corresponding to only 16 possible global signal configurations. For a problem of this size, the global optimum can be obtained exactly through exhaustive classical enumeration. Consequently, the manuscript should report the exact minimum Ising energy obtained by exhaustive search and compare it directly with both the classical heuristic and the QAOA solution. This would establish (i) whether QAOA actually converges to the global optimum, (ii) the probability that QAOA recovers the true optimum across repeated stochastic runs, and (iii) the optimality gap when it does not. Without this benchmark it is difficult to assess the actual optimization performance of the proposed quantum algorithm. Furthermore, because exhaustive optimization is computationally trivial for this network, it represents the appropriate gold standard rather than merely another optional comparison.

The manuscript would also benefit from evaluating the methodology on a larger traffic network. While a 5×5 grid would certainly be informative, I believe that extending the study to at least a 3×3 grid would provide the most appropriate balance between computational feasibility and scientific value. A 3×3 network already increases the optimization problem substantially while remaining sufficiently tractable for comparison against strong classical optimization methods. Demonstrating that the proposed framework continues to provide benefits beyond the smallest non-trivial network would considerably strengthen the manuscript’s claims regarding practical applicability. If evaluation on a larger grid is beyond the intended scope of the current work, this limitation should at least be discussed explicitly.

Another important concern is the evaluation of QAOA under an entirely idealized quantum computational model. The current simulations assume perfect quantum operations and do not consider gate infidelity, measurement errors, decoherence, depolarizing noise, parameter uncertainty, circuit connectivity constraints, transpilation overhead, or other realistic characteristics of present-day quantum hardware. The issue is not that the algorithm is simulated rather than executed on physical hardware—simulation is entirely appropriate for methodological development—but rather that the manuscript provides no assessment of how sensitive the reported performance gains are to deviations from ideal quantum execution. The conclusions would therefore be strengthened considerably by a systematic sensitivity analysis in which synthetic quantum noise is introduced into the simulation. Examples include gate perturbations, measurement noise, depolarizing channels, phase-flip or bit-flip errors, or parameter uncertainty in the QAOA rotation angles. Such analyses would determine whether the reported improvements remain robust under realistic execution conditions or whether they depend critically upon idealized assumptions.

Related to this, the manuscript currently fixes the QAOA hyperparameters without demonstrating the robustness of the reported results. Performance should ideally be evaluated over a range of QAOA parameters (e.g., rotation angles, circuit depth, and measurement shots) to demonstrate that the observed improvements do not depend upon a particular parameter selection. Similarly, sensitivity analyses varying the number of measurement shots would provide insight into the stability of the optimization procedure.

A further issue concerns the relationship between the Ising objective function and actual traffic performance. The manuscript implicitly assumes that minimizing the pressure-based Ising Hamiltonian necessarily translates into improved traffic metrics such as waiting time, travel time, or throughput. However, no analysis is presented demonstrating that lower Ising energies consistently correspond to improved network performance. It would therefore be valuable to quantify the relationship between optimized Ising energy and downstream traffic metrics through correlation or regression analyses. Such an analysis would validate the proposed objective function itself rather than merely the optimization algorithm applied to it.

The statistical analysis should also be strengthened. At present the manuscript relies primarily upon averages, standard deviations, and significance testing across repeated simulation runs. Given that QAOA is itself a stochastic optimization algorithm, I recommend reporting bootstrap confidence intervals for all principal outcome measures, including waiting time, travel time, throughput, and energy reduction. Confidence intervals on the improvement attributable to QAOA would provide substantially more information than p-values alone. In addition, reporting effect sizes (e.g., Cohen’s d, Hedges’ g, or appropriate non-parametric alternatives) would allow readers to assess the practical significance of the observed improvements rather than relying exclusively on statistical significance. Distributional visualizations such as boxplots or violin plots would further illustrate the variability of the optimization outcomes.

Because the exact global optimum is known for the current network, an especially informative statistical measure would be the probability that QAOA recovers the global optimum, together with bootstrap confidence intervals for this probability. Alternatively, the manuscript could report the distribution of the optimality gap between QAOA and the exact optimum across repeated runs. These metrics would directly evaluate the optimization algorithm itself rather than relying solely on downstream traffic metrics.

Finally, the manuscript would benefit from broader benchmarking against established adaptive traffic-signal control methods. Although the current comparisons against fixed-time, queue-based control, and the proposed pressure-based controller are useful, additional comparisons with established adaptive controllers such as Max-Pressure, SCOOT, SCATS, PressLight, CoLight, MPLight, or other state-of-the-art adaptive traffic signal algorithms would place the proposed methodology within the context of the existing literature and provide a clearer indication of its practical significance.

Overall, I believe the proposed pressure-based Ising formulation is scientifically interesting and the manuscript has considerable potential. However, the current evidence does not yet isolate the contribution of the quantum optimization algorithm from the broader benefits of global optimization, nor does it quantify the robustness of the reported improvements to realistic quantum uncertainties. Incorporating an exact classical global optimization benchmark, extending the evaluation beyond the current 2×2 network, performing sensitivity analyses under synthetic quantum noise, strengthening the statistical analyses with bootstrap confidence intervals and effect sizes, evaluating the probability of recovering the true optimum, and validating the relationship between Ising energy and traffic performance would substantially increase the rigor, credibility, and long-term impact of the study.
